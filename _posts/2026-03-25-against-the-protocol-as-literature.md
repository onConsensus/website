A small genre has emerged over the last two years of essays that propose to
read foundational protocol documents — the Bitcoin whitepaper, the Ethereum
yellow paper, sundry rollup whitepapers — as *literature*. The framing is
intended to flatter. It is not flattering, and it is not, on closer reading,
even meant to be.

## What the argument actually says

The argument runs roughly as follows. These documents are widely read. They
are quoted with reverence. They have a recognizable house style — clipped,
declarative, mathematical, occasionally Latinate. Therefore, the essays
conclude, we ought to read them as *texts*: with attention to genre, to
voice, to rhetorical posture.

It is the conclusion that does the political work. To read a whitepaper as
literature is to release it from the obligation to be true.

## Two kinds of literary reading

It is worth distinguishing between two ways one might read a whitepaper as
literature, because the genre conflates them.

The first is **forensic**. One reads a technical document as a literary
object the way an editor reads a manuscript: looking for what is said and
what is conspicuously not, where the metaphors are doing analytical work
and where they are doing concealment. This is a useful posture. We will use
it again in this magazine.

The second is **devotional**. One reads a technical document as a literary
object the way one reads a sacred text: with the assumption that the
pleasure of the reading is itself the point, and that the document's
relationship to its claims is a question for somebody else. This is the
posture the recent essays adopt, even where they would deny it.

> The forensic reading sharpens the document. The devotional reading
> dissolves it.

## Why this matters

The protocols described in these documents are not literature. They are
machines that hold money. The texts that describe them are not poems. They
are specifications, with all the ordinary obligations that specifications
carry: they should be testable, falsifiable, and accountable to the
running code.

Treating them otherwise is not generous. It is a way of insulating their
authors from the responsibilities that any other engineering document
would carry. A bridge is not made safer by a beautiful drawing. A protocol
is not made trustworthy by a beautifully written whitepaper.

## A modest proposal for reviewers

For the reviewer of a forthcoming whitepaper, two questions to keep on the
desk:

1. Where does the prose substitute for the proof?
2. Where does the manifesto substitute for the engineering plan?

The honest documents survive the questions. The dishonest ones do not. The
literary essays we have been reading do not ask either.
