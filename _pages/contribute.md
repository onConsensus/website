There are four ways to contribute to On Consensus. Every one of
them assumes you have read the [ethos](/ethos/) and the
[colophon](/colophon/) first.

## Pitch a piece

We are a small desk and we publish slowly. We commission roughly
two longform pieces and one or two dispatches per week, across all
six sections. We are reachable.

**What we want.**

- Reporting and analysis on protocols, clients, and the
  organizations around them. Specific, checkable, and dateable.
- Cryptographic primer essays where the working cryptographer is
  the writer, not the editor.
- Governance pieces that take the politics seriously, including the
  small-p politics of working groups, foundations, and standards
  bodies.
- Post-mortems of incidents, deployments, and migrations. Boring
  is good. Numbers are good.
- Criticism — books, conferences, papers, code, the field's prose.
- Dispatches: link-blog items with one paragraph of editorial.

**What we don't want.**

- Price commentary or anything that resembles trading advice.
- Project announcements, launch coverage, or anything that reads
  like a press release in the second paragraph.
- Generative-model output, in whole or in part. See the
  [AI policy](/ethos/#ai).

**How.** Send a one-page outline to `pitches@onconsensus.com`. Tell
us what the piece is, why it has to exist now, what you can prove,
who you have talked to, and roughly how long it will be. We answer
within seven days. If we say yes, we agree on a length, a deadline,
and a fee before you start writing. We do not pay on spec.

**Rates.** Equal per-piece rates across contributors, depending on
length and reporting depth. The current rate card is published
under the masthead at [/authors/](/authors/) and on the editorial
GitHub repository.

**Editorial process.** First read by the section editor; second
read by a different editor; line edit; fact check (we do our own —
contributors who do not enjoy the fact-check process are not the
right fit). The author signs the piece off in writing before
publication. We do not publish under contributor bylines without
contributor approval of the final text.

**Ownership and licence.** Authors retain copyright. We publish
under [CC-BY-SA-4.0](/colophon/#licensing) by default. Authors who
need a different licence — for republication into journals or for
licensing constraints from prior work — should flag it in the pitch.

## File a correction

Every article has a *Suggest an edit* link at its foot, going
straight to the source on GitHub. That is the fastest path: open a
pull request that proposes the change, and we will respond on the
PR.

For substantive corrections — a load-bearing fact, a misquote, a
misattribution — write to `corrections@onconsensus.com` with the
URL of the piece, the specific claim, and the evidence you would
have us check against. The
[corrections policy](/ethos/#corrections) is the contract.

## Send a tip

Encrypted mail to `tips@onconsensus.com`, against the editorial PGP
key in the [colophon](/colophon/).

We treat tips with care. We will not name a source we agreed not to
name. We will tell you if we cannot publish, and we will tell you
why. We do not republish tips wholesale; we report.

If you are reaching out from inside an organization that may be
monitoring its egress, prefer Tor and a fresh PGP keypair, and
expect us to verify both keys out of band before we ask you for
anything specific.

## Support the publication

We accept reader support directly, without intermediaries who would
collect tracking data on you. The most useful kinds of support, in
order:

1. **Read us.** Subscribe to the [Atom feed](/feed.xml). Forward
   pieces. Send us corrections.
2. **Pay us.** Recurring contributions via the channels listed at
   [/colophon/#funding](/colophon/#funding). Suggested rates are
   modest; one-off contributions are also welcome.
3. **Pitch us.** The piece you wish you'd read this week is
   probably the piece we wish we had this week.

We do not run paywalls. We do not run "members-only" content. The
publication is free to read and free to mirror, in perpetuity,
under the terms of the licence.
