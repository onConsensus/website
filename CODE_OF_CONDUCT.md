# Code of Conduct

On Consensus is a small, public publication. The repository is open so that
readers can verify our work and contribute corrections, pitches, and code.
What follows is the standard we hold ourselves and contributors to in this
repository, on the issue tracker, and in pull-request discussion.

## The standard

We commit to making participation a harassment-free experience for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

Examples of behaviour that contribute to a positive environment:

- **Argue the work, not the person.** Direct, technically detailed criticism
  is welcome and expected. Ad hominem is not.
- **Cite, don't insinuate.** If you are claiming a fact, link a source.
- **Disclose conflicts.** Holdings, employment, advisory roles, prior work.
  Disclosure is treated as a sign of seriousness, not a black mark.
- **Assume the contributor is a real person reading in good faith** — until
  the evidence says otherwise.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- Sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any
  kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information — physical or email address,
  on-chain identity tied to a real name they have not disclosed, employer —
  without explicit permission.
- Threats, doxxing, brigading, coordinated abuse, or impersonation.
- Bad-faith engagement designed to consume reviewer time without producing
  editorial value (so-called "JAQing-off", sealioning, gish-gallops).

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all repository spaces — issues, pull
requests, discussions, the Decap CMS editorial workflow — and in any space
where an individual is representing the publication.

## Enforcement

The editorial team is responsible for clarifying and enforcing the standard.
Editors will take whatever action they judge appropriate, up to and
including: editing or removing comments, locking issues, closing pull
requests without merge, and banning contributors temporarily or permanently
from the repository and any subsequent contributions.

Enforcement decisions are not subject to appeal in public threads. They may
be appealed by writing to **editors@onconsensus.com** with a short
explanation; we will respond within seven days.

## Reporting

Email **editors@onconsensus.com**. We will treat your message as
confidential. If you are uncomfortable mailing the editorial alias, write
directly to a named editor on the masthead.

If you believe an editor has acted in bad faith, write to a different
editor. The publication is small but it is not a single person.
